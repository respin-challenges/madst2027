Data

New sentences in nine Indian languages, each translated by people into the other eight and read aloud. Every development and test sentence has human reference text and human reference speech in all nine languages.

At a glance

Languages
Nine: Bengali, Bhojpuri, Chhattisgarhi, Hindi, Kannada, Magahi, Maithili, Marathi and Telugu, giving 72 translation directions
Original sentences
About 20,500 unique sentences, between 2,000 and 3,000 per language, in standard dialects
Splits
About 16,000 training, 1,800 development and 2,700 test sentences
Human translations
About 164,000: every original sentence is translated into the other eight languages
Speakers
270, 30 per language, balanced by gender: 14 for training, 6 for development and 10 for test in each language
Utterances
81,000 newly recorded read-speech utterances, 300 sentences per speaker

How the data is built

  1. Original sentences. Each language contributes between 2,000 and 3,000 original sentences in its standard dialect, about 20,500 in total.
  2. Translation. People translate every original sentence into the other eight languages. An original and its eight translations form a sentence group: the same sentence in all nine languages.
  3. Recording. Speakers read the sentences aloud: 30 speakers per language, balanced by gender, 300 sentences each.
  4. Splitting. Groups are divided into training, development and test sets. A group always stays in one split, so no sentence, in any language, appears in both training and test data. No speaker appears in more than one split.

In each direction

Take any direction, for example Bhojpuri to Kannada:

  • Development: 1,800 sentences, each with human reference text and human reference speech.
  • Test: 2,700 sentences, with references withheld until the challenge ends.
  • Training: parallel text for all training groups. Many training groups are also recorded in several languages, giving real speech-to-speech pairs.

Related corpora

Open-track systems may use any public corpora. The challenge description names these as examples:

  • RESPIN-S1.0, a read speech corpus of over 10,000 hours in dialects of nine Indian languages (Kumar et al., NeurIPS 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks Track, doi:10.52202/085713-4928). See also the RESPIN project.
  • SYSPIN_S1.0, a TTS corpus of over 900 hours in nine Indian languages (Abhayjeet et al., “SYSPIN_S1.0 Corpus – A TTS Corpus of 900+ hours in nine Indian Languages”, 2025). Available from the SYSPIN corpus page.
